# Where to look

Web search and fetch cover most of the market; Reddit goes through reddit_search.py (reddit.md). Open every page you cite.

## Competitors

- **Searches** → "<the job> software", "<the job> app for <customer>", "alternatives to <leader>", "<leader> vs"
- **Category pages** → software review sites' category lists name who's in the market, even when the reviews are paywalled
- **App stores** → the top results for the job, with rating counts as a traction signal
- **The leader** → the biggest tool the customer already uses that does part of this, even if it isn't a direct rival

## Pricing

- **The competitor's own pricing page** → plans, price per seat or use, what the free tier holds
- **No public price** → "contact sales" is itself a signal: they sell to larger customers
- **Date it** → put the date read in the source line; prices change

## Traction

- **Public signals** → funding announcements, press, job posts, review and rating counts, app store rank
- **Paywalled data** → funding databases and analyst reports show snippets at most; mark anything from them as an estimate

## Failed attempts

- **Searches** → "<category> shut down", "<category> startup post-mortem", "sunsetting <product>", "why we shut down" with the job's keywords
- **What to record** → what it was, when it stopped, and the stated reason; the reason is the lesson
- **Acquired and closed** → counts as a failed attempt unless the buyer kept the product

## Demand

- **Reddit** → counts of customers asking for help with the job (reddit.md)
- **Workarounds** → templates, spreadsheets, and freelancers sold for the job show people pay to solve it
- **Hiring** → job posts for someone to do the job by hand show what it costs today

## Complaints

- **Competitors' reviews** → the one- and two-star reviews, grouped by what repeats
- **Reddit** → comment search for the leaders' names in the customer's subreddits
- **What to record** → the complaint in the customers' words, and how often it repeats
