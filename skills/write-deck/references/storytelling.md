# Storytelling

How a deck's argument is built, before any slide is designed. The rules come from people who studied what audiences remember and act on:

- Barbara Minto, *The Pyramid Principle*: answer first, then grouped support
- Michael Alley, *The Craft of Scientific Presentations*, and Garner & Alley's assertion–evidence studies: a sentence title over visual evidence beats a topic title over bullets, for both understanding and recall
- Richard Mayer, *Multimedia Learning*: coherence, signaling, redundancy, contiguity, segmenting
- Nancy Duarte, *slide:ology* and *Resonate*: the big idea, the glance test, the shape of a persuasive talk
- Gene Zelazny, *Say It With Charts*, and Cole Nussbaumer Knaflic, *Storytelling with Data*: one message per chart
- Edward Tufte, *The Cognitive Style of PowerPoint*: what bullet outlines do to reasoning

## Start from the ask

- **Before any slide** → write `Audience:` and `Ask:` in one line each
  - _Because:_ a deck is a means to a decision; without the ask, every slide is equally (un)important
- **The ask won't fit one sentence** → the deck isn't ready; ask the requester what they want to happen
  - _Because:_ Duarte's big idea is one sentence: your point of view and what's at stake
- **Audience is "everyone"** → name the most skeptical person in the room and write for them
  - _Because:_ the doubts you answer are the doubts of someone specific

## Answer first

- **Ordering a business deck** → the answer or ask by slide 2 or 3, then the support
  - _Because:_ Minto: a busy audience decides early; a mystery structure loses them before the reveal
- **Opening** → situation (what they agree with), complication (what changed), then the answer
  - _Because:_ SCQA earns the right to give the answer; skipping it makes the answer feel unmotivated
- **Grouping support** → 2–4 groups that don't overlap and together cover the answer
  - _Because:_ an audience holds about three things; overlapping groups make them hold the same thing twice
- **Deck has 3 or more groups and 10 or more slides** → a `section` slide opens each group
  - _Because:_ signposts let people find their place; a short deck doesn't need them
- **Persuading a skeptical crowd** → alternate what is and what could be, then call to action
  - _Because:_ Duarte's sparkline: contrast creates the tension that a plain list of benefits lacks

## Titles carry the story

- **Writing a content title** → a full-sentence claim: subject, verb, what's true
  - ✗ `Q3 revenue` ✓ `Q3 revenue beat plan by 12%, led by enterprise`
  - _Because:_ assertion titles tell the audience what to see; topic titles make them guess
- **Checking the storyline** → read only the titles, in order; they must make the whole argument
  - _Because:_ horizontal logic: if the titles alone don't persuade, the slides won't either
- **A title only makes sense after its body** → rewrite it until it stands alone
  - _Because:_ the audience reads the title first and interprets the body through it
- **A title needs "and"** → it's two slides
  - _Because:_ one claim per slide; two claims split attention and neither gets proved
- **Tone of a title** → plain and specific; the evidence makes it interesting
  - ✗ `It's not a pricing problem. It's a trust problem.` ✓ `Churned customers cite trust twice as often as price`
  - _Because:_ dramatic framing reads as generated and triggers skepticism in exactly the people you need

## The body proves the title

- **Choosing a layout** → pick by the evidence's type, not for variety
  - one number → `big-number`; quantities over time or across groups → `chart`; how parts connect → `diagram`
  - two or three options → `comparison`; many attributes → `table`; a customer's words → `quote`
  - the thing itself → `image`; steps in time → `timeline`; a short list nothing else fits → `points`
  - _Because:_ assertion–evidence: the title says it, the visual shows it
- **Something on the slide isn't evidence for the title** → cut it
  - _Because:_ Mayer's coherence principle: extra material, even interesting, lowers what's remembered
- **The slide has text the presenter will also say** → move it to the notes
  - _Because:_ Mayer's redundancy principle: reading and listening to the same words compete
- **Highlighting** → one accent on the one thing that matters: the bar, the number, the row
  - _Because:_ signaling: the eye goes where contrast is; everything highlighted means nothing is
- **Glance test** → a presented slide's point lands in about 3 seconds
  - _Because:_ Duarte: if they're reading, they aren't listening

## When to split, when to merge

- **Split** → the title needs "and"; two charts; body over the layout's word cap; a list past 4; a step in a sequence
  - _Because:_ Mayer's segmenting: people learn more from several simple slides than one dense one
- **Merge** → two neighbours make the same claim; a slide exists only as a transition
  - _Because:_ every slide costs attention; a slide with no claim spends it for nothing
- **Detail the skeptic might ask for** → `Not saying`, or an appendix only if the requester wants one
  - _Because:_ backup data in the main flow buries the argument

## Length

- **Presented deck** → about one content slide per minute; never past 1.5
  - _Because:_ a slide needs time to land and be discussed; more slides means each one is skimmed
- **Slot length** → plan to talk for 75% of it
  - _Because:_ questions and a late start eat the rest
- **Read deck** → as many slides as the argument needs, each with full sentences
  - _Because:_ a read deck replaces the presenter; its slides carry what the notes would

## Close on the ask

- **Last slide** → the ask again, as a concrete next step with an owner or date
  - ✗ `Thank you` or `Questions?` ✓ `Approve two senior hires by Nov 1 to hold the Q1 launch`
  - _Because:_ the closing slide stays up through questions; it should keep working

## Speaker notes

- **Presented deck** → each slide's `<aside>` holds what the presenter says, in full sentences
  - _Because:_ the slide shows evidence; the notes carry the explanation the slide left out
- **Writing notes** → conversational, as spoken; end with the bridge to the next slide
  - _Because:_ notes are read aloud under pressure; written prose stumbles when spoken
