# Hard questions

What a skeptical investor asks of each section. A question the brief can't answer from its own items and market.md becomes a finding: a blocker when the brief rests on it, a note when it doesn't.

## Per section

- **Customer** → Could the founder name ten of them? Who pays, and is that the person who uses it?
- **Problem** → How often does it happen, and what does it cost today? What have they already tried?
- **Alternatives** → What do they do now, including nothing? Why would they switch, and what do they give up?
- **Solution** → Is it 10x better on what the customer cares about, or slightly better at everything?
- **Why now** → What changed? Why didn't this work five years ago, and why won't the leader do it now?
- **Market** → Is the count bottom-up and sourced? Are those customers reachable, or only out there?
- **Model** → Who pays how much, and what's left after hosting, AI calls, fees, and support?
- **Channels** → Where do the first 100 come from, by name? Can the price pay for that channel?
- **Edge** → What gets harder to copy each month? What stops the leader shipping it as a feature?
- **Why you** → What does the founder know or reach that a funded team doesn't?
- **Assumptions** → Is the riskiest one first? Can each test fail, and is the bar set before it runs?
- **First version** → Does each feature test an assumption? Is there anything here only because it's fun to build?

## Checking the numbers

- **Size** → customers × price per year; check each factor against its source, and the multiplication
- **Unit economics** → LTV at least 3× CAC, and CAC earned back within 12 months, for the brief's channel
- **Margin** → for an AI product, estimate the AI cost per customer per month against the price
- **Ambition** → a venture brief needs a path past roughly $100M a year; a bootstrapped one needs margin from the first customers

## Pre-mortem

Imagine it's two years later and the product failed. Write the three likeliest reasons, then check the brief answers each:

- **Nobody cared enough** → the pain was real but too small or too rare to act on
- **They cared but wouldn't pay** → free alternatives were good enough, or the buyer wasn't the user
- **Couldn't reach them** → the channel cost more than a customer was worth
- **The leader shipped it** → a feature in a tool they already use ended the reason to switch
- **Couldn't build it well** → the hard part was harder than the brief assumed

## Common failures

- **Builders mistaken for demand** → the Reddit evidence is mostly founders asking the same question
- **Survey intent as evidence** → "80% said they'd use it" settles nothing; ask what they did
- **Top-down sizing** → a share of a big market instead of a count of reachable customers
- **The spreadsheet ignored** → the brief compares itself to startups while customers use a spreadsheet and are fine
- **Vitamin, not painkiller** → nice to have; nobody has tried to fix it
