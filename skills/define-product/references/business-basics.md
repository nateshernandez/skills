# Business basics

What each brief section must answer, the bar each ambition sets, and how to rate the verdict. Written for a technical founder seeing these terms for the first time; the numbers are common rules of thumb, not laws.

## Ambition sets the bar

- **`venture`** → investors need a path to a very large outcome, roughly $100M+ in yearly revenue
  - Size must be large bottom-up; Edge must explain why the leader can stay the leader
  - A small, happy market is a fail here, however good the product
- **`bootstrapped`** → profitable early on the founder's money; growth paid for by customers
  - Model must show margin from the first customers; Channels must be cheap or free
  - Size needs only enough reachable customers for the founder's income goal
- **`indie`** → a small product run by one or two people; a side income or a lifestyle business
  - Channels and Why you matter most; Size and Edge barely do
- **Ask, never assume** → the same idea can be a fine indie business and a bad venture pitch

## Customer

- **The beachhead** → the narrow group you win first, specific enough to name ten of them
  - ✗ "small businesses" ✓ "solo bookkeepers with 20 to 60 clients"
- **Buyer vs user** → in businesses, who pays often isn't who uses it; name both when they differ
- **Where they gather** → the subreddits, forums, and groups market.md found them in; Channels uses this

## Problem

- **Pain** → how often it happens, what it costs in hours or money, and what they've tried
- **Hair on fire vs nice to have** → people already paying or hacking a workaround is the strongest sign
- **No alternative at all** → often means the pain is too small to act on, not an open market

## Alternatives

- **The real competition** → what customers do today, including spreadsheets, hiring someone, or nothing
- **Switching cost** → what they'd give up to move to you: data, habits, integrations, retraining
- **Competitors' complaints** → repeated complaints in reviews and threads are the gap a new product fills

## Solution and why now

- **10x on one thing** → better at what the customer cares about most, not a little better at everything
- **Why now** → a change that opens the door: a new technology, a law, a platform shift, a competitor leaving
  - "AI can do this now" counts only if the brief says why the incumbents' AI can't

## Market size, bottom-up

- **Method** → reachable customers × price per year; cite where each number comes from
  - Example, with made-up numbers: 40,000 reachable bookkeepers × $348 a year = $13.9M a year
- **Top-down is a warning sign** → "1% of a $50B market" tells an investor you haven't counted customers
- **Reachable** → customers your channels can reach, not everyone with the problem

## Model and unit economics

- **Pricing models** → per seat, per use, flat tiers, or a cut of transactions; match how the customer gets value
- **Anchor on alternatives** → what customers pay today for competitors or workarounds is the ceiling to beat
- **Gross margin** → revenue minus the cost of serving it (hosting, AI calls, payment fees, support)
  - Software usually keeps 70 to 80%; AI-heavy products often keep less, so model the per-customer AI cost
- **CAC** → what it costs to win one customer (ads, sales time, content), averaged
- **LTV** → what one customer is worth: monthly margin × the months they stay
- **Rules of thumb** → LTV at least 3× CAC, and CAC earned back within 12 months

## Channels

- **The first 100** → name the place and the move: "post in r/Bookkeeping's weekly thread", not "social media"
- **Common channels** → founder-led sales, communities, content and search, marketplaces, partnerships, the product spreading itself
- **Channel fit** → a $10 a month product can't afford sales calls; a $50,000 a year one needs them
- **One or two** → a young product wins with one channel done well, not five done thinly

## Edge

- **What compounds** → network effects, data only you collect, switching costs, distribution, brand
- **Feature, not product** → if the leader can ship it in a quarter, the brief must say why they won't
- **None yet** → honest and common at the start; say what would build one

## Why you

- **Founder fit** → years in the industry, access to customers, an insight others miss, a skill that's rare
- **Grade** → usually `stated`; investors check it in conversation

## Rating the verdict

- **Pain** → strong when customers pay or hack around it today; weak when threads are mostly founders asking
- **Reach** → strong when one or two channels hold most of the beachhead and you can name them
- **Willingness to pay** → strong when people pay for alternatives at your price; unknown until someone quotes a price
- **Gap** → strong when competitors' repeated complaints match what the Solution does
- **Edge** → strong when something compounds; weak when a leader could copy it in a quarter
- **Why you** → strong when the founder has access or insight others lack
- **Size** → judged against the ambition's bar above, never against an absolute number
